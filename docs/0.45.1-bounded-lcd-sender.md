# 0.45.1 — bounded board-specific LCD transmission

User testing of 0.45.0 reported severe interaction lag. UART showed a 234810 us
maximum UI snapshot publication and a 303907 us background frame. The pixel
diff engine and its potentially large sequence of tiny transactions are removed
from this build, including the third-party source and extra mirror/diff buffers.

The sender now consumes LVGL damage rectangles. It merges damage when pending
frames are replaced or a newer frame supersedes selected unsent work. Small
changes upload their bounding rectangle; changes covering at least one quarter
of the display upload one complete image. Both paths have at most 24 pixel DMA
transactions, with two private bounce buffers and confirmed completion before
reuse. Transfer errors force a complete repaint on the next available image.
Failed final DMA drains retain ownership and stop further publication.

The UI retains its compose/latest-pending/sending ownership isolation and can
replace a waiting frame while SPI is busy. Large transfers can adopt a newer
completed snapshot after their background entry wait. There is no pixel-wise
PSRAM comparison or canonical mirror/transposition pass. Landscape full frames
use 24 logical vertical strips following physical scan order, directly packed
from the logical snapshot. Small rectangles use ordinary logical row chunks.

Only large frames use scan feedback. The board measures its ST7796 scan period
after configuring FRMCTR1 near 50Hz. Each entry uses a validated pair of reads,
at most one task sleep, and at most one further validation pair. Corrupt reads
have a fixed three-attempt bound. No microsecond busy-wait or polling-until-
window loop remains. Calibration is bounded and runs on the background task;
failed calibration retries no more often than every two seconds. Small region
updates never enter this wait and are not claimed to be scan-synchronized.

Validation:

- `python tools/test-lcd-async.py`: exact cropped pixels, preservation of unsent
  damage when promoting/replacing frames, 640-byte single-row update, 3162-byte
  merged partial update without scan waits, 24-strip landscape upload, callbacks,
  orientation epochs, allocation failures and confirmed DMA ownership.
- `python tools/test-lcd-frame-window.py`: all modeled scan phases, no more than
  four normal reads / one sleep <=22ms, bounded corrupt-feedback rejection, and
  calibration recovery. Startup calibration is separate from the per-frame bound.
- IDF build, COM10 application-only flash with hash verification, normal boot.
- Real Canvas workload: 200 frames each at 8/32/64/96 moving sprites. All four
  stages completed with zero runtime errors and heap check `ok=1`. Highest
  observed UI publication time was 7224 us. Last periodic benchmark report:
  791 full frames synchronized, 0 late, 0 send errors. Those counters are the
  last recorded sample, not an assertion about every possible future workload.

Artifacts: `dist/firmware/lcd-bounded-0451/manifest.json`; timed workload evidence
`.tools/diagnostics/lcd-bounded-performance-20261021-145802/`.
The benchmark generated moving Canvas sprites, not game play or page swipes.
Input latency was not measured electrically; physical tearing remains a visual
acceptance test for the user. The algorithm prioritizes bounded execution and
responsiveness; it does not promise tear-free output for all partial updates.
